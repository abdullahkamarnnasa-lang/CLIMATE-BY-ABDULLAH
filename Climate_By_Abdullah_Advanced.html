🛰️ Why NASA Observes Earth

NASA uses satellites, aircraft, ground observations and scientific models to study Earth's atmosphere, oceans, land, ice and ecosystems.

Long-term observations let scientists compare conditions across years and decades, helping identify climate trends, changes in ice, sea level, ocean heat and atmospheric gases.

Climate science combines observations from many independent systems rather than relying on one measurement.

How Do We Know Climate Change is Real?

Earth-orbiting satellites and other observation systems measure temperature, ice, oceans, land and atmospheric composition around the world.

When these independent records are combined over long periods, they reveal persistent changes in Earth's climate system.

What is Causing Climate Change?

Human activities have increased the concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere, especially through fossil-fuel use and changes in land use.

Greenhouse gases absorb outgoing infrared radiation, strengthening the natural greenhouse effect.

What Are the Effects of Climate Change?

Observed effects include warming temperatures, glacier loss, sea-level rise, changing precipitation patterns and increasing heat risks in many regions.

Impacts vary by location and can affect people, ecosystems, water resources, agriculture and infrastructure.
