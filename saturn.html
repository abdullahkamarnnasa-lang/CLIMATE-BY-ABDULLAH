THE SIXTH PLANET FROM THE SUN

SATURN

The spectacular gas giant famous for its enormous ring system. Explore its atmosphere, storms, rings, moons, history and the missions that revealed this extraordinary world.

Saturn at a Glance

Saturn is a giant world made mostly of hydrogen and helium. It is the second-largest planet in our Solar System and has a density lower than water.

6th
Planet from the Sun

Saturn orbits far beyond Jupiter, at an average distance of about 1.4 billion km from the Sun.

Equatorial diameter

Its wide equator and rapid rotation make Saturn noticeably flattened at the poles.

Approximate day

A Giant World of Gas

There is no solid surface like Earth's. As you descend through Saturn, gas becomes denser and eventually behaves like a deep, hot fluid under immense pressure.

☁️ Atmosphere

Hydrogen dominates Saturn's atmosphere, with helium and smaller amounts of other compounds. Pale cloud bands wrap around the planet.

🌪️ Extreme Weather

Saturn has powerful jet streams, enormous storms and a famous hexagonal pattern around its north pole.

🧊 Deep Interior

Pressure rises dramatically toward the center. Models indicate a dense core surrounded by metallic hydrogen and molecular hydrogen layers.

💨 Fast Winds

Upper-atmosphere winds can reach hundreds of meters per second, far faster than typical winds on Earth.

The Famous Rings

Saturn's rings are not a single solid disk. They are made of countless particles of water ice and rocky material, ranging from tiny grains to much larger pieces.

💍 A, B & C Rings

The bright A and B rings are separated by the Cassini Division. The dimmer C ring lies closer to Saturn.

🧊 Mostly Ice

Ring particles are dominated by water ice, which makes the rings exceptionally reflective and bright.

🌑 Gaps & Waves

Saturn's moons gravitationally shape the rings, opening gaps and creating waves and intricate patterns.

🔭 Changing View

Because Saturn is tilted, the rings appear to change angle from Earth's perspective during its orbit.

A Family of Moons

Saturn has a remarkable collection of moons, including worlds with oceans, thick atmospheres, active geology and strange landscapes.

🌊 Titan

Saturn's largest moon has a thick nitrogen-rich atmosphere and lakes and seas of liquid hydrocarbons on its surface.

💧 Enceladus

A bright icy moon that sprays plumes of water-rich material into space. Evidence points to a subsurface ocean.

🪨 Iapetus

Known for its dramatic two-tone appearance, with one hemisphere much darker than the other.

⚪ Rhea

A heavily cratered icy moon with a bright, reflective surface and a complex history.

Exploring Saturn

Humans have studied Saturn for centuries with telescopes and spacecraft. Robotic missions transformed it from a distant point of light into a complex planetary system.

1610 — Galileo observes Saturn

Early telescopic observations revealed unusual structures around Saturn, although the rings could not yet be understood.

1655 — Titan discovered

Saturn's largest moon was discovered during the early era of telescopic astronomy.

1979–1981 — Pioneer 11 & Voyagers

Flybys returned close-up observations of Saturn, its rings, atmosphere and moons.

1997 — Cassini–Huygens launches

The international mission began a journey to Saturn and arrived in 2004.

2004–2017 — Cassini era

Cassini orbited Saturn for more than a decade, while the Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005.

2017 — Grand Finale

Cassini completed a series of close dives between Saturn and its innermost rings before the mission ended.

The North-Pole Hexagon

One of Saturn's strangest atmospheric features is a long-lived six-sided jet-stream pattern surrounding the north pole. Its enormous scale makes it unlike ordinary weather systems on Earth.

⬡ A Giant Atmospheric Pattern

The hexagon is a persistent wave in Saturn's polar jet stream. Different spacecraft and telescope observations have shown changes in its colors and surrounding atmospheric conditions over time.
