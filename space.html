🌙 Moon & Major Moon Worlds

Some moons are active worlds with volcanoes, thick atmospheres, ice shells or subsurface oceans.

Earth's Moon

Diameter ≈3,475 km. A rocky satellite heavily marked by craters and large impact basins.

Notable terrain: Olympus-sized mountain comparisons are not appropriate; the Moon has enormous peaks and crater rims created mainly by impacts.

Ganymede

Jupiter's largest moon and the largest moon in the solar system.

Science interest: evidence points to a subsurface ocean.

Europa

Jupiter's icy moon with a strong scientific case for a subsurface ocean.

Mission: NASA's Europa Clipper is designed to study its habitability.

Io

Jupiter's volcanically active moon. Tidal heating powers intense geological activity.

Titan

Saturn's largest moon and the only moon known with a thick atmosphere.

It has surface lakes and seas of methane and ethane.

Enceladus

Saturn's icy ocean moon. Jets of water-rich material erupt from fractures near its south pole.

⛰️ Mountains, Volcanoes & Height / Topography

Here are notable planetary relief features. “Height” can depend on the reference datum used, so planetary topography is best treated as a measurement with a defined baseline.

Mars — Olympus Mons

Height: ≈27 km above the surrounding reference surface.

Base: more than 600 km across.

NASA describes it as the largest known volcano in the solar system.

Venus — Maxwell Montes

Elevation: about 11 km above the mean planetary radius reference.

It is the highest mountain range on Venus, within the Ishtar Terra region.

Earth — Mount Everest

Elevation: ≈8.85 km above mean sea level.

Earth's highest point above sea level.

Moon — Mountainous Highlands

Large impact basins, mountain ranges, crater rims and peaks dominate much of the ancient highlands.

Mapping: spacecraft laser altimetry provides detailed elevation maps.

What is a heightmap?

A heightmap stores elevation values across a surface as a grid. Planetary scientists build digital terrain models from laser altimeters, stereo images, radar and other observations. Bright/dark pixels can represent high/low terrain depending on the dataset.

☄️ Dwarf Planets, Asteroids, Comets & Outer Regions

Pluto

Complex Kuiper Belt dwarf planet with mountains, valleys, plains, craters and glaciers. Reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006.

Moons: 5 known major moons, including Charon.

Ceres

Largest object in the main asteroid belt and the only dwarf planet in the inner solar system. NASA's Dawn spacecraft visited it.

Radius: ≈476 km.

Haumea

A rapidly rotating dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt with an elongated shape and two known moons.

Makemake

A distant Kuiper Belt dwarf planet covered in volatile ices such as methane.

Eris

A massive dwarf planet in the distant solar system and one of the objects that helped drive the debate that led to Pluto's reclassification.

Asteroid Belt

The main belt lies between Mars and Jupiter. Most asteroids orbit the Sun there, and they are remnants of early solar-system formation.

Kuiper Belt

A thick disk of icy bodies beyond Neptune, beginning near ~30 AU. Its main region extends to roughly ~50 AU, with the scattered disk reaching farther.

Oort Cloud

A theorized spherical shell of icy bodies from roughly 5,000 to 100,000 AU. It has not been directly observed.

🚀 Human Spaceflight & Robotic Exploration

Voyager 1 & 2

The two Voyagers are the only spacecraft to have reached interstellar space; their science continues to inform studies of the outer heliosphere.

Perseverance — Mars

Explores Jezero Crater, studies ancient environments and collects rock samples for possible future return.

Curiosity — Mars

Studies Mount Sharp and Gale Crater to understand Mars' ancient environments and habitability.

Europa Clipper

NASA mission designed to make repeated close flybys of Europa and investigate its ice shell, ocean, chemistry and geological activity.

New Horizons

Flew past Pluto in 2015 and later explored a Kuiper Belt object, expanding knowledge of the outer solar system.

Webb Space Telescope

Observes infrared light from distant galaxies, stars, planets and exoplanet atmospheres, extending space science far beyond the solar system.

✨ Exoplanets & Deep Space

NASA Exoplanet Archive

The NASA Exoplanet Archive reported 6,354 confirmed planets on 20 August 2026, with more candidates and confirmations added over time.

Exoplanets are worlds orbiting stars beyond our Sun. Scientists study their size, mass, orbital periods, host stars and atmospheres to understand how planetary systems form and where conditions might permit life.

🧠 Space Facts

Why is Venus hotter than Mercury?

Venus has a dense atmosphere dominated by carbon dioxide, producing an extreme greenhouse effect that traps heat efficiently.

How big is Saturn's ring system?

NASA notes the visible ring system spans roughly 280,000 km while being extremely thin compared with its width; some measured sections are only around meters to tens of meters thick.

How far is the Oort Cloud?

NASA places its theorized region at about 5,000–100,000 AU, far beyond the Kuiper Belt.

Can planets have rings?

Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune have ring systems; NASA notes at least one asteroid also has rings.
