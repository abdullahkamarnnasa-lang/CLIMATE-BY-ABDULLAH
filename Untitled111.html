Understanding Arctic Sea Ice Minimum Extent

What is Arctic Sea Ice?

Arctic sea ice is frozen seawater that covers the Arctic Ocean and surrounding areas.

The amount of sea ice changes throughout the year. It usually reaches its smallest area near the end of summer.

Arctic Sea Ice Minimum Extent

~4.3 million km²

Recent September minimum extents have been among the lowest observed in the satellite record.

Values vary from year to year.

Why Does Arctic Sea Ice Matter?

🌡️ Climate

Bright sea ice reflects sunlight back into space and helps regulate Earth's climate.

🐻‍❄️ Wildlife

Arctic sea ice provides important habitat for polar bears, seals and other wildlife.

🌊 Oceans

Changes in sea ice affect ocean circulation and Arctic ecosystems.

What Happens When Ice Decreases?

Less sea ice means more dark ocean water is exposed. Dark water absorbs more sunlight than bright ice, which can contribute to additional warming.

This is known as the ice-albedo effect.

Effects of Arctic Ice Loss

  • ❄️ Less reflective ice
  • 🌡️ Increased regional warming
  • 🐻‍❄️ Loss of wildlife habitat
  • 🌊 Changes in Arctic ecosystems
  • 🌍 Wider impacts on the climate system
